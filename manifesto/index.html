The Manifesto

Nature enters history as a subject.

The founding text of The Embassy of Nature.

For centuries, nature was present in every one of our decisions and absent from the table where they were made.

It sustained our cities. It fed our economies. It shaped our cultures and the identity of nations. We learned to count with precision the value of what could be extracted from it; we left unrepresented the value of its remaining alive.

That age is coming to an end.

Nature has begun to be recognised as a subject of rights. That affirmation transforms our idea of justice. It also obliges us to ask a question the world has not yet fully answered: who carries its voice to the places where its future is decided?

That is why TEON exists — The Embassy of Nature.

We were born to give institutional form to a presence that should never have been missing: to carry the rights of nature into the conversation among nations, into culture, into the economy, and into the decisions that will define this century.

TEON develops a new form of functional government: an organisation of responsibilities and capacities so that nature has a voice in the matters that affect it. The authority of that form of government will grow through public rules, verifiable work, and cooperation agreed with states. Its vocation does not stop at borders, because the life it seeks to represent does not stop there either.

A declaration of rights opens the way. What follows requires institutions able to give those rights expression, agreements able to sustain them, and an economy able to recognise them. The protection of nature must have real consequences in the way the world decides and creates value.

Environmental capitalism

We want to build conditions in which preserving can compete economically with destroying. A living forest, a healthy ocean, and a reserve that remains intact must be able to count in the future of a country.

Cultural capitalism

The identity, memory, and imagination of nations are living capacities. When they find a platform worthy of them, they create presence, work, opportunity, and prosperity without losing their meaning.

Natural sovereignty

The capacity of a nation to decide upon its patrimony without economic urgency forcing it to exhaust it. A country must be able to conserve and to prosper. Its communities must be able to take part in that future.

The young

The young are inherent to this agenda. They will live longest with the consequences of today’s decisions and will help create the institutions that are still missing. Their place is at the table where it is defined how nature is represented, how its value is recognised, and what future we build with it. TEON convenes them as authors, with voice and responsibility from the outset.

Our origin

TEON was founded in 2023 from the historical tradition of the House of Lises. That history gives us a starting point and a responsibility: to build an institution that outlasts its founders, able to cooperate with states and to answer publicly for its acts.

We work for the recognition of nature as a subject of rights in the international order as well, and for the institutional forms capable of representing its interests. This is an agenda to be built with states, communities, and those who give shape to law and to the economy.

Our vocation is to give nature an institutional place in the world. Its form is built through thought, culture, agreements, verifiable work, and new generations prepared to take it on. We do not wait for every answer to be written before beginning that work.

We are here to affirm that nature has rights. To create the conditions in which those rights are heard. To show that preserving can generate prosperity. To gather those who understand that the future needs new institutions and have the will to build them.

Nature has rights. Now it has an Embassy.
